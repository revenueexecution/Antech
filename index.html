Step 1

Executive Summary

How I commercialise complex technology and turn technical capability into customer outcomes.

This methodology demonstrates how I would organise knowledge, qualify opportunities and create a repeatable commercial approach in a technical sales environment.

Why I Built This

I wanted to create more than interview notes. This is a practical demonstration of how I think: understand the customer context, identify the operational or financial problem, connect it to the right technical solution and build a credible value case.

Commercial Philosophy

Lead with the problem

Understand the process, risk and business impact before discussing technology.

Translate complexity

Convert technical features into operational, financial and compliance outcomes.

Build trust

Use application knowledge and disciplined discovery to become a credible adviser.

Create momentum

Agree clear next steps, stakeholders, proof requirements and decision criteria.

My Commercial Framework

  1. Understand the market and account context.
  2. Identify the customer problem and buying trigger.
  3. Quantify the operational, financial or compliance impact.
  4. Align the right product, application and internal expertise.
  5. Build and validate the value case.
  6. Progress the opportunity through agreed next steps.
I commercialise complex technology.
Presentation note
Open by explaining that this is a methodology, not a finished system. It shows how you would approach the role.

Key Takeaways

  • The customer problem comes before the product.
  • Technical credibility must lead to measurable value.
  • Commercial discipline makes the approach repeatable.

Step 3

ReSolve Commercial Case Study

A value-led example showing how I would turn a technical product into a business case.

Executive Summary

ReSolve should be positioned as a business-improvement investment rather than simply a piece of laboratory equipment. The commercial case combines solvent purchasing, hazardous-waste disposal, handling, sustainability and exposure to price volatility.

The Customer Problem

  • High solvent purchasing cost
  • Hazardous-waste disposal and transport cost
  • Manual handling and storage burden
  • ESG and waste-reduction pressure
  • Capital scrutiny requiring a clear value case

Illustrative Value Case

£250kIllustrative annual solvent spend
70%Illustrative recovery assumption
£175kIllustrative gross saving before operating cost
Important qualificationActual solvent suitability, recovery rate, operating cost, capital cost and payback must be validated using customer data.

Discovery Questions

  • Which solvents and methods drive the highest annual spend?
  • What volume is purchased, consumed and discarded?
  • What are the disposal, transport and handling costs?
  • Which methods are technically suitable for recovery?
  • What documentation and validation evidence would QA require?
  • What operational constraints could affect adoption?
  • Who owns the budget and business case?
  • What payback threshold is used?
  • What competing projects could delay approval?
The value becomes compelling when the customer sees the combined solvent, waste, handling, sustainability and risk benefit.

Key Takeaways

  • Start with the economics and operating problem.
  • Validate technical suitability before presenting ROI as fact.
  • Build one combined business case across multiple value drivers.

  • Continuous or high-volume manufacturing
  • Sustainability commitments and published targets
  • Capital-investment or capacity-expansion programmes

Step 10

30-60-90 Day Plan

Moving from learning and alignment to qualified pipeline and territory execution.

Days 1–30

Learn & Align

  • Complete portfolio and application training
  • Build internal stakeholder relationships
  • Review CRM, pipeline and territory history
  • Shadow customer meetings
  • Validate priority markets and accounts

Outputs

Learning plan, stakeholder map and territory baseline.

Days 31–60

Engage & Qualify

  • Segment accounts
  • Launch priority commercial plays
  • Run discovery meetings
  • Develop early value cases
  • Coordinate demonstrations and technical proof

Outputs

Qualified opportunities and initial account plans.

Days 61–90

Build & Execute

  • Progress priority opportunities
  • Establish forecast rhythm
  • Build account-expansion plans
  • Present territory strategy
  • Define the next-quarter focus

Outputs

Pipeline, forecast cadence and next-quarter plan.

Success Measures

  • Confident product, application and market conversations
  • Targeted prospecting and meaningful customer engagement
  • Qualified opportunities with value, stakeholders and next steps
  • Accurate CRM and disciplined forecasting
  • Strong internal collaboration

Key Takeaways

  • The first 30 days establish credibility and focus.
  • The next 30 days test the methodology in customer conversations.
  • By 90 days, learning should translate into pipeline and execution rhythm.
